# Crit 10 Reflection

## What was the breakthrough that moved the work forward?

I did not complete every requirement of Crit 10 by its deadline, but I did try using two different browsers to simulate students accessing ANU TaskMate independently.

This changed how I thought about the system. In Crit 9, I had mainly focused on whether updates appeared across users without refreshing. With two browser sessions, I started considering a different question: what should each person actually be allowed to see or do?

A post owner and an applicant may be looking at the same task, but they should not have identical permissions.

My attempts were an early exploration rather than a completed demonstration of all the identity, authorisation, and logging requirements. I explored those issues more thoroughly during the later Final Project development, when I tested application management and protected contact sharing.

## What did this work change about who I want to be as a software developer?

This experience helped me understand that multi-user software is not only about synchronising information. It also requires clear boundaries between users, especially when private messages or contact details are involved.

I want to design from the perspectives of different participants, test realistic workflows myself, and avoid assuming that a feature is safe simply because the interface looks correct.

Later work reinforced the importance of server-side permission checks and human judgement alongside AI-generated code.
